# Family interview questions

Use a few questions per visit, record the conversation with their agreement, and let them wander when a good memory surfaces. The answers can become stories for a book or audiobook.

## Start with their voice

1. What would you like your children or grandchildren to know about you?
2. If you were telling them where your story begins, where would you start?
3. Is there a story about you that the family tells often? How do you remember it?
4. Are there parts of your life you’d rather keep private?

## The world they grew up in

5. What was your home like when you were little? Take me through it.
6. Who was around the table when you were growing up? What were they like?
7. What did an ordinary day look like then?
8. What sounds, smells, foods, or sayings take you straight back to childhood?
9. What did you love doing? What got you into trouble?
10. Who made you feel safe, or helped you become yourself?

## How life unfolded

11. What did you imagine your life would be like when you were young?
12. Tell me how you met someone who became important to you.
13. How did you choose your work, or how did it choose you?
14. What was a time when everything seemed to change? What happened that day?
15. What was one of your happiest periods? What made everyday life good?
16. What was a hard period, and what helped you through it?
17. Is there a choice you made that still surprises you?
18. What did you give up to care for someone or pursue something important?

## The family story

19. What do you remember about becoming a parent, grandparent, or the role you have had in this family?
20. What family tradition would you like us to carry on? Where did it come from?
21. Who in the family should we know better through your stories?
22. What do you remember about *[a shared event]*? I’d love to hear it from your point of view.
23. What have we always misunderstood or never thought to ask?

## Looking back

24. What are you proud of that might not appear in a list of accomplishments?
25. What did you believe when you were younger that you see differently now?
26. What have the people you love taught you?
27. Which moments would you most want included in your story?
28. What would you like to say directly to the people who will read or hear it?

## Follow-up prompts

When they begin a story, try asking: **“Tell me what happened next.”** Then ask who was there, what the place looked like, what was said, and how they felt *at the time*. These details help make a scene work on the page and in audio.

Before using a recording, ask plainly: “Are you comfortable with us recording this and using parts of your words or voice in a family book or audiobook? Is anything from today just for us?” Keep photographs and letters nearby as memory prompts, and record how names and places are pronounced.
